Student pitches

  • p. 29 ↗ Short First Solar By Kirill Aleksandrov 2015 Amici Capital Prize, finalist

    Kirill argues First Solar was over-earning on legacy projects and on demand pulled forward before a solar tax credit expired.
  • p. 31 ↗ Long JetBlue Airways By Harry Garcia 2015 Amici Capital Prize, finalist

    JetBlue's returns on capital had trailed its rivals for three years. Harry's case rests on what had just started to change.
  • p. 33 ↗ Long Schibsted By Luke Tashie 2015 Amici Capital Prize, finalist

    A 175-year-old Oslo media company turning into a leading online classifieds operator in more than 20 countries.
  • p. 35 ↗ Long CDK Global By Brian Waterhouse 2015 Amici Capital Prize, finalist

    A classic spin-off from ADP, earning far less than its closest competitor, Reynolds & Reynolds.
  • p. 37 ↗ Short SolarCity By Brendan Dawson, Sisy Wang and Aaron Purcell Darden @ Virginia Investing Challenge, 1st place

    A leader in solar installation whose valuation, the team argues, ignores real risks: little brand or cost advantage over peers, plus the step-down in the solar tax credit.
  • p. 39 ↗ Short Cardtronics By Damian Creber, Edward Reynolds and Kevin Lin Alpha Challenge at UNC Kenan-Flagler, 1st place

    7-Eleven was 24% of revenue and 45% of EBITDA, and after depreciation and interest, nearly all of the profit. That contract was under attack.
  • p. 41 ↗ Long Progressive Waste Solutions By Matt Brownschidle, Winston Hu, Jay Ju and Steve Lin Ross Investment Competition (University of Michigan), 2nd place

    US landfills fell from about 8,000 in 1988 to about 2,000 in 2014, so the ones left are worth more.
